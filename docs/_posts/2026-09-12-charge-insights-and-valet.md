---
title: Charge insights and a calmer parked-car screen
description: The home screen now separates driving, parking, charging, and valet time without turning the dashboard into a trip report.
---

The dashboard is most useful when it answers the question in front of the
driver. While driving, that is energy use and battery expenditure. Once parked,
the car should feel settled rather than continue presenting a trip as if it were
still in progress.

This change introduces a small journey card that follows that rhythm. Driving
keeps efficiency and battery change prominent, with distance and duration as
supporting detail. Parking gets its own stable state, including an active charge
when the car is connected. The detailed records remain in the statistics screens,
where they are useful without competing with the immediate task.

## Charging is a parked activity

Charging now belongs to the parking stay in which it happened. The charge history
continues to record each session, while the home screen can state the practical
result: the battery change and elapsed charging time. The charging history also
has a reachable curve view for the people who are curious about how a session
developed over time.

## Valet is deliberate and persistent

Valet mode is treated as one deliberate interval: it starts after the owner
parks, stays visible overnight or through a restart, and ends only when the
owner explicitly closes it. Its compact card is meant to make the state obvious
without pretending it is a security lock screen. The interval is also recorded
alongside daily sessions so that the history remains coherent.

## The next step

This is deliberately the simple version. A future valet lock or PIN can become a
real access boundary only when it is designed as one. For now the important
property is continuity: parked state, charging, and valet time survive long
enough to be useful when returning to the car later that night.
